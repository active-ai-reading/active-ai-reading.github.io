## Weekly blog writing tips
where there are multiple posts for the same role, try to find points where different posts conflict; these are probably the most interesting things to highlight. Especially true for covering the critiques. Did someone praise a control while someone else found it to be not sufficient? did someone praise the novelty while someone else called the ideas unoriginal? Highlight the core difference and where it stems from.

## Contacting the authors

### When to reach out
After you've read the paper carefully and something is still unclear. First reread the relevant section and check the appendix, supplementary material, code repository, and any public reviews or discussion.

### Who to write to
- Address the first author and the corresponding author together (the corresponding author is marked on the first page). The first author often knows the implementation details best, and the corresponding author can speak to the bigger picture.

### Good questions to ask
- Clarifications of a method, an assumption, or a detail that isn't specified (hyperparameters, data splits, preprocessing).
- Questions about a result you couldn't interpret or reproduce.
- Limitations or failure cases they saw but didn't have space to report.
- Whether a gain comes from the proposed idea or from a confounding factor

### Before you send
- Note exactly where you got stuck (section, figure, table).
- Keep it to one to three focused questions.
- Give enough context that the authors don't need to reread the paper to answer.
