This guide is yours to write. Some questions to start from (answer them, replace them, ignore them):

- How do you critique fairly: what to look for first, what to ignore?
- How do you find a paper's ancestors and recent relatives quickly?
- What makes a follow-up idea worth doing?
- How did contacting authors go, and what would you say differently?
- What did you try that didn't work?

## Starting points

Collected by the instructor to help the first people in this role. Keep what helps, add what you find.

- **Critique:** [paper-sharpener](https://github.com/borgr/paper-sharpener)'s *paper review* skill (Claude Code) first checks a paper against itself (numbers against tables, claims against results), then simulates three reviewers of different seniority. Run it, then decide which of its points you actually agree with. Your judgment is the post. For papers about evaluation, the [LLM evaluation tutorial](https://github.com/borgr/tutEval) lists what to check: prompts, compute, metrics, human evaluation.
- **Literature review:** ["Find related papers"](https://borgr.github.io/paper_updated/#find-related-papers) in *paper_updated* covers citation-graph tools (Connected Papers, Research Rabbit, Litmaps) and forward and backward citation search. The "Related Work" section of the [writing guide](https://docs.google.com/document/d/14Wax8M5w8F_8miDlYJ9-I6wqpelxlXjCEUbkNzNMqqE/edit?usp=sharing) says what a good related-work story does: position through concrete differences, and credit.
- **Follow-ups:** the "Analysis" and "Conclusion and discussion" sections of the [writing guide](https://docs.google.com/document/d/14Wax8M5w8F_8miDlYJ9-I6wqpelxlXjCEUbkNzNMqqE/edit?usp=sharing) describe what a paper owes its readers about implications and future work, which is a good lens for spotting what's missing.
- **Examples on this site:** a [critique]({{ '/posts/2026/critique/' | relative_url }}), a [literature map]({{ '/posts/2026/literature-map/' | relative_url }}) and [five follow-ups]({{ '/posts/2026/follow-ups-worth-doing/' | relative_url }}).
