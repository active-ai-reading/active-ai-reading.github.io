## About the paper

Finetune an LLM on new facts and it often seems to forget what it knew before. Previous work showed that some of this forgetting is spurious: the old knowledge is still in the model, just hidden, and can be recovered. This paper asks when and why that happens. It finds that recall of old facts can collapse, then come back on its own as training continues, before a slower, permanent erosion sets in. The authors identify three ingredients for the spurious part: old facts queried in similar ways, new answers concentrated in one part of the vocabulary, and a normalization layer.

## Our posts:

**✍️ Writing.** [Rewriting the abstract](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/abstract-spurious-forgetting-b1wa/) puts the paper's main point first, that forgetting during finetuning is two processes and only one is permanent, and replaces unexplained jargon with plain language. A [copyedit of Section 2](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/copyedit-spurious-forgetting-8vno/) introduces the two settings and the experiment before the results, shows the evidence that the old knowledge is hidden, not erased, and ends with the questions the experiment raises.

**🧠 Learning.** To understand how a model can lose access to facts without erasing them, we first look at [where facts are stored](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/teach-spurious-forgetting-q0l2/). The post summarizes work showing that a transformer's feed-forward layers act like key-value memories: each neuron detects a pattern in the input and pushes the model toward certain next words, and the final prediction combines many such memories.

**📊 Visualization.** [A better Figure 1](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/redo-graph-spurious-forgetting-s2ol-adamg/) removes redundancy: since one finetuning scenario is a special case of the other, it keeps a single panel and cleans up the rest, labeling the lines directly and shading the pretraining phase as context. Two redesigns of Figure 3 then explain why old facts come back: a [schematic of the three phases](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/better-figure-3-spurious-forgetting-yak0v/), with and without normalization, showing where old and new facts land and which are answered, and an [interactive story](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/a-better-figure-3-spurious-forgetting/) in which a shared shortcut helps the model learn the new facts but hides the old ones, then becomes harmful, so training pulls it back and the old facts reappear without being retrained. Two redesigns of Figure 5 show the evidence that removing a single direction restores old facts: [grouped bars](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/redo-graph-spurious-forgetting-9xtc/) make the size of the recovery easy to compare against a random-direction control, and a redesign with [one panel per experiment](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/better-figure-5-spurious-forgetting/) draws ordinary finetuning and the edit side by side, and adds the cost of the edit on the new facts, which the original left out.

**🔬 Research.** A [literature review](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/literature-spurious-forgetting/) places the paper where four lines of work meet, from catastrophic forgetting to recoverable forgetting, and points to the paper that coined "spurious forgetting" as the one to read first. Two posts follow the authors' path, [one on Palit and Schölkopf](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/spurious-forgetting-authors-path-yakovafgin/) and [one on all five authors](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/spurious-forgetting-authors-path-Shueyr/), from interpretability, causality and the study of how models learn facts to this paper. Two critiques ask how far the results go: [one on the toy model](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/critique-spurious-forgetting/) questions whether the simplified model and synthetic data match real LLMs, and [the other on the OLMo results](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/critique-spurious-forgetting-chanoch1234/) asks why OLMo recovered old facts in only one of two experiments and proposes a cleaner test. [Three follow-ups](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/followups-spurious-forgetting-chenshani-droid/) propose experiments on semantic structure, abilities beyond facts, and preventing the shift through training order.

**💻 Coding.** [A ten-seed reproduction of Figure 2](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/coding-nathan/) rebuilds the paper's toy memory and shows that the more the facts share, the sharper the collapse of old facts, followed by recovery and erosion. [A rerun of Figure 1](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/reimplement-spurious-forgetting-biography-wording/) reproduces the paper's two finetuning scenarios, then rewrites the new facts in a different phrasing and finds that the wording changes what gets forgotten, and that the paper's explanation does not cover every case.

**🔭 Scouting.** The scouting posts look for recent work on the paper's question: when is lost knowledge really gone? [Five papers on forgetting during finetuning](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/hidden-gems-spurious-forgetting-4vdo/) start from the paper that named spurious forgetting and cover why reinforcement learning forgets less, when replay is enough, and how pretraining length matters. Two posts find the same pattern of hidden, not erased knowledge elsewhere: [facts removed by editing a model](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/scouting-not-trendding-spurious-forgetting/) can still be read from inside it, and [information supposedly removed by unlearning](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/hidden-gems-spurious-forgetting-glmf/) hides in intermediate layers. Two more look at [ways to forget less](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/trending-spurious-forgetting/), such as deleting a tiny fraction of weights, and at a trend where much of "training" turns out to be [a single shift](https://active-ai-reading.github.io/posts/2026/trending-spurious-forgetting-wge3/), which echoes the paper's shared shift.
